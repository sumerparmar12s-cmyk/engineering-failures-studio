# AMOC: Earth's conveyor belt

## Narration (final)

Just imagine if a giant conveyor belt that keeps Europe warm... suddenly started slowing down.

And scientists say it's already happening. It's called the AMOC.

You see, this belt is made of ocean currents. Warm water from the Gulf of Mexico flows north, past the U.S. East Coast, all the way to Europe.

That's why London is much milder than Calgary, even though it's farther north.

Near Greenland, it cools, gets heavier, and sinks deep, then flows back south along the ocean floor. Around and around, like a belt.

But melting ice from Greenland pours fresh water into the North Atlantic. Fresh water is lighter, so it doesn't sink as easily. And the belt slows down.

If it weakens a lot, Northern Europe could get much colder winters, sea levels could rise faster on the U.S. East Coast, and rain belts could shift in Africa and South America.

And it's happened before. About 12,900 years ago, a slowdown like this is thought to have plunged the North back into near ice age cold, for over a thousand years.

Today, some studies suggest the AMOC is the weakest in a thousand years. And no one knows exactly when, or if, it could hit a tipping point.

## Claim check
- AMOC (Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation) includes the Gulf Stream / North Atlantic Drift; warm surface water north, deep water formation in the Labrador/Nordic seas, return flow at depth (NOAA, Met Office).
- London (51.5N) mild vs Calgary (51.0N): London is ~0.5 deg farther north; Calgary much colder winters (continental + no ocean heat).
- Greenland meltwater freshening reduces density and sinking (IPCC AR6).
- Projected impacts of a strong weakening/collapse: colder N. Europe winters, faster sea-level rise on US East Coast, shifts of tropical rain belts (IPCC AR6, Met Office).
- Younger Dryas ~12,900 years ago, abrupt return to near-glacial conditions for ~1,200 years; AMOC slowdown is a leading hypothesis ("is thought to").
- Caesar et al. 2018/2021: AMOC weakest in over a millennium (proxy); significant scientific debate ("some studies suggest"); tipping-point timing uncertain.
