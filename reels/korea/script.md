# The Korean War

## Narration (final)

Just imagine: a small country gets invaded by its neighbor. Within weeks, America, Britain, Canada and more than a dozen others rush in with their armies. And then... an even bigger country joins the war.

After World War Two, Korea was split in two. The North was backed by the Soviet Union. The South, by the United States. Two governments, and each one wanted all of Korea.

June 25th, 1950. North Korean tanks roll across the border. In just days, Seoul falls. South Korea's army is pushed all the way back to a small corner in the southeast.

But America wasn't coming alone. Under the United Nations flag, troops pour in. Britain, Canada and New Zealand send soldiers. And Norway sends a medical team to treat the wounded.

Then, a huge twist. UN forces land by sea at Inchon and take back Seoul. Victory looks close... until China jumps in, and pushes them back south.

Now this isn't just a fight between two Koreas. The world's biggest powers are tangled up in it.

Finally, on July 27th, 1953, an armistice stops the fighting. Korea stays divided. And the wildest part? To this day, the two Koreas have never signed a peace treaty.

## Claim check
- Korea divided at the 38th parallel in 1945 (Soviet occupation north, US south); two governments in 1948, each claiming the whole peninsula.
- June 25, 1950: North Korean invasion with T-34 tanks; Seoul fell June 28; UN/ROK forces pushed into the Pusan Perimeter (southeast).
- UN Security Council resolutions; 16 nations sent combat forces (incl. UK, Canada, New Zealand); Norway sent a mobile army surgical hospital (NORMASH).
- Inchon landing Sept 15, 1950; Seoul retaken late Sept; China entered Oct-Nov 1950 and pushed UN forces back south of Seoul.
- Armistice July 27, 1953; no peace treaty has been signed.
