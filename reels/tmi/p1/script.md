# Three Mile Island · Part 1 · The Light That Lied

## Narration (final)

At four in the morning, a light in a nuclear control room told the operators a valve was closed. It was lying.

And that one light pushed America into the worst accident in the history of its nuclear power.

March 28th, 1979. Three Mile Island, Pennsylvania.

It starts small. The pumps feeding water to the steam generators stop. The turbine shuts down. Then the reactor shuts itself down too.

Pressure rises, and a relief valve opens to let it out. That's normal. When the pressure drops, the valve should close.

It doesn't. It's stuck open.

But in the control room, the instruments say it's closed.

So cooling water, escaping as steam, keeps pouring out. And nobody knows.

Then comes the worst move of all. The operators think the system has too much water. So they cut back the emergency cooling water.

Inside the reactor, the water level falls. The core starts to overheat.

Before that day was over, part of the core would melt.
