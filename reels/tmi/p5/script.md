# Three Mile Island · Part 5 · The Bubble

## Narration (final)

Inside a crippled nuclear reactor, a bubble was growing. And some of America's top experts feared it could explode.

This is part five of Three Mile Island.

By Friday, engineers at the Nuclear Regulatory Commission had found something new. A large bubble of hydrogen gas had formed at the top of the reactor vessel.

One of the agency's leading safety experts, Roger Mattson, gave the chairman the bad news. The bubble stays.

It couldn't simply be pushed out. Lowering the pressure would only make it bigger.

The question that scared everyone: if oxygen mixed in, could it burn, or even explode?

The agency later estimated that if enough oxygen had been there, the energy would have been about the same as a thousand pounds of TNT.

For two days, officials argued. The bubble was on the news across America.

Then, on Sunday afternoon, NRC engineer Victor Stello found the mistake. The scary calculation had used the wrong formula.

Hydrogen needs oxygen to burn. And inside the reactor, there was no free oxygen.

The bubble that terrified a nation could never have exploded.

But that Sunday, a very important visitor was already on his way in.
