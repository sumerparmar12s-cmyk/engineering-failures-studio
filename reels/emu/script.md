# The Great Emu War

## Narration (final)

Just imagine: an army marches out with machine guns and ten thousand bullets... to fight a bird. And the bird wins.

Western Australia, 1932. About twenty thousand emus are raiding the wheat farms.

[am_adam] Hello? Government? The birds are eating everything!

And the government says: send in the army.

[bm_george] How hard can it be?

Major Meredith shows up with two machine guns and ten thousand rounds.

[bm_lewis] Gentlemen, this will be over by lunch.

The soldiers open fire... and the emus split up and run in every direction. Like they rehearsed it.

Plan B: an ambush at a watering hole. Hundreds of emus walk right in. The gun fires... and jams.

Plan C: put the gun on a truck. The emus are faster than the truck.

After a week: twenty-five hundred bullets. Just a few dozen birds.

The army keeps trying until December. Then the mission is called off. Later, farmers just got paid a bounty per emu instead.

And history gave it a name: the Great Emu War. Final score? Emus: one. Army: zero.

## Claim check
- Nov-Dec 1932, Campion district, Western Australia wheatbelt; ~20,000 emus damaging crops; farmers (many ex-soldiers) petitioned the government.
- Major G.P.W. Meredith, Royal Australian Artillery, two Lewis guns, 10,000 rounds.
- Nov 2: emus scattered out of range. Nov 4: ambush near a dam with ~1,000 emus; gun jammed after about a dozen killed. Gun mounted on a truck; emus outpaced it on rough ground.
- By Nov 8 about 2,500 rounds fired; kills estimated from about 50 to 200 ("a few dozen" kept conservative). Withdrawn Nov 8, resumed Nov 13, ended Dec 10. Bounty system later.
- Minister/farmer/major lines are clearly comic dramatisations.
