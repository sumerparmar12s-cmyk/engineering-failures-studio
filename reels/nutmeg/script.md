# Manhattan for nutmeg

## Narration (final)

In 1667, the Dutch gave up Manhattan... and in return, they kept this tiny island. That's not a joke.

And at the time? It looked like a great deal. Let's rewind.

In the 1600s, Europe was obsessed with one spice: nutmeg. It was so valuable, sailors risked their lives crossing half the planet for it.

And it grew in only one place on Earth: the tiny Banda Islands, in today's Indonesia.

The Dutch grabbed almost all of them. Except one, called Run. The English got there first.

Meanwhile, on the other side of the world, the Dutch had a little trading town called New Amsterdam.

In 1664, English warships showed up. And the Dutch governor, Peter Stuyvesant, peg leg and all, surrendered without a fight.

The English renamed it New York, after the king's brother, the Duke of York.

Three years later, the peace treaty said: everybody keeps what they've got. So the Dutch keep Run, and the English keep Manhattan.

The Dutch: nutmeg forever! The English: enjoy your island.

Fast forward. Nutmeg now grows all over the tropics. And Manhattan is worth... a little more.

So next time you sprinkle nutmeg on your latte, remember: it cost the Dutch New York.

## Claim check
- Nutmeg native only to the Banda Islands (Moluccas, Indonesia); Dutch VOC took control of the Banda Islands; the English held Run (Nathaniel Courthope, 1616-1620).
- New Amsterdam on Manhattan; English fleet arrived 1664, Peter Stuyvesant (who had a wooden leg) surrendered without bloodshed; renamed New York after James, Duke of York, brother of King Charles II.
- Treaty of Breda (1667): status quo (uti possidetis): England kept New Netherland/New York, the Dutch kept Run (and Suriname).
- Nutmeg later transplanted to other tropical regions (e.g., Grenada, Zanzibar, Sri Lanka) in the late 18th-19th century.
