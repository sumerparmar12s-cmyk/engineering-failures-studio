# Point Roberts: America you can only drive to through Canada

## Narration (final)

This is the United States. And this is Canada. Now look closely at this tiny corner.

This little piece is America... but you can't drive there without going through Canada.

Welcome to Point Roberts, Washington.

How did this happen? Back in 1846, America and Britain had to split the land out west. And they picked the easiest option: one long, straight line.

The 49th parallel. Clean. Simple. Done.

Except... nobody really checked what was under the line.

It sliced right through this peninsula, and the tip landed on the American side.

Britain, probably: can we just keep that bit? America: Nope. A line is a line.

So today, about a thousand Americans live here, boxed in by Canada and the sea.

High school kids ride a bus through Canada, crossing an international border four times a day.

Meanwhile, Canadians drive in for cheaper gas, and to pick up their online orders.

So if you live in Point Roberts and forget your passport... you're basically on an island.

## Claim check
- Oregon Treaty (1846) set the US-British boundary west of the Rockies along the 49th parallel; the tip of the Tsawwassen peninsula south of it became Point Roberts, WA (Whatcom County), reachable by land only through Canada (AP, 2017).
- Population ~1,200 (about a thousand).
- Older students attend Blaine schools; the trip crosses into Canada and back into the U.S. each way (AP; JSIS UW).
- Canadians buy cheaper gas and use parcel-receiving businesses in Point Roberts (widely reported).
- "Britain, probably..." is a clearly signalled joke, not a quote.
