# Short 3 — The last line of defense

## Narration (final)

A four-hundred-ton machine on the seafloor had one job: seal the well in an emergency. It failed. Here's why.

When the Deepwater Horizon blew out in 2010, the crew's last hope was the blowout preventer. A steel tower, fifty-seven feet tall, sitting a mile underwater. Its blades were built to cut through the drill pipe, and seal the well shut.

After the explosions, a crew member hit the emergency disconnect. Nothing happened. The explosions had likely cut the lines.

An automatic backup likely did fire. But the pipe inside had buckled, bent off-center by the force of the flowing well. And the blades couldn't seal it.

Oil poured into the Gulf for eighty-seven days. Full story on our channel. Should these machines be tested more often?
